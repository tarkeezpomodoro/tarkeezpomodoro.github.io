    What is the Pomodoro Technique?

    The Pomodoro Technique is a simple time-management method created by Francesco Cirillo in the late 1980s. You split your work into short focus intervals (usually 25 minutes) separated by short breaks. Each interval is called a "pomodoro" — Italian for "tomato" — after the tomato-shaped kitchen timer Cirillo used as a student.

    How to use it (5 steps)

    1. Pick one task
    2. Set the timer to 25 minutes
    3. Work only on that task
    4. Take a short 5-minute break
    5. After 4 rounds

    Why it helps people who struggle to focus

    • Starting feels easy

      Just 25 minutes is far less daunting than "hours of work", so procrastination drops.

    • It fights distraction

      A clear end point helps your brain postpone distractions instead of giving in to them.

    • It prevents burnout

      Regular breaks refresh mental energy and keep your focus sharp all day.

    • It shows your progress

      Counting completed pomodoros gives you a sense of achievement and keeps you going.

    Tips for better results

    • Silence phone notifications — or leave the phone in another room — during a focus round.
    • Break big tasks into small ones that take 1–4 rounds each.
    • If 25 minutes feels too long, start with 15 in Settings and build up gradually.
    • Don't scroll your phone on breaks; move or breathe deeply so your mind actually rests.
    • If you're interrupted, finish the round later or restart it — a pomodoro is indivisible.

    FAQ

    Are my tasks and data saved?

    Yes — only in your browser (local storage). Nothing is sent to a server. Clearing your browser data removes it.

    Does the timer keep running if I switch tabs?

    Yes. The timer stays accurate in the background and shows the remaining time in the tab title. Turn on notifications in Settings to get alerts.

    Is it free?

    Yes — completely free, and no sign-up needed.
