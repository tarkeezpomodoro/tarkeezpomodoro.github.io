How to Beat Distraction and Poor Focus with the Pomodoro Technique

Updated: November 25, 2026 · 2 min read

One notification, a passing thought, an urge for a "quick look" at your phone… and suddenly thirty minutes are gone with nothing done. Distraction isn't laziness or weak willpower — the brain naturally drifts toward whatever is easier and more stimulating. The fix isn't forcing yourself to focus for hours; it's structuring your time so focus becomes easier.

Why a timer helps you focus

When a task has no clear end, the effort feels huge, so your mind escapes to distractions. A timer draws a clear boundary: "I work for 25 minutes, then I rest." That lowers the pressure and makes it easier to postpone distractions, because you know they'll get their turn soon.

Five practical strategies

  1. Keep a "later" list: have a sheet of paper next to you. Whenever something pops up (a message, a search, an idea), write it down and get straight back to work. Deal with it during the break.
  2. Start with a shorter round: if 25 minutes feels hard right now, set the timer to 10 or 15 minutes in Settings. What matters is finishing the round — then lengthen it gradually.
  3. Physically remove the biggest distraction: a phone in another room beats a phone face-down on the desk.
  4. Make the first step tiny: "Open the file and type the title" is enough to break the starting barrier — you'll often keep going on your own.
  5. Use sound: some people find a soft ticking sound helps them stay in work mode. You can turn it on in Settings.

What if a round gets interrupted?

It happens to everyone. If something important interrupts you, reset the timer and restart the round later. Don't be hard on yourself — the goal is gradual improvement, not perfection.

A note on ADHD

Many people with ADHD find that breaking time into short, defined intervals helps them get started and keep going. But this technique is a support tool, not a treatment. If poor focus is significantly affecting your studies, work, or daily life, it's worth talking to a doctor or specialist.

The bottom line

You don't need superhuman willpower — you need a system that makes focus easier. Start right now with one short round on the most important task you've been putting off.
